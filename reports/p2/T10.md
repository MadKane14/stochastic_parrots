## What I did
- v2 of `p2_translit`: learned abbreviation canonicalization (competitive linking on unshared tokens, order-preserving
  abbreviation ≤ 5 letters, long form not a compound) and learned junk tokens; off by default (v1 configs unchanged).
- `exploration/p2/dense_cv_group.py`: p1's dense CV restricted to one country-string group (exact for that group:
  blocker and features work per group) so it fits Kaggle's 31 GB. `exploration/p2/canon_report.py`: held-out map check.
- Configs on top of p1's T08_sub: `T10_d` (v1), `T10_e`, `T10_f` (v2); `T13_*` (+ p4_e5); `T14_full` (full team stack).

## What I learned
- p2 v1 is the biggest single gain in the dense-CV table on India (+0.0079) at unchanged candidate counts (33.3/S1);
  both precision and recall rise, singleton accuracy 0.9879 → 0.9901.
- Address abbreviation learning is real and safe (true-pair overlap +0.128, random +0.006, 51% of true pairs improve)
  and lifts US blocking recall +0.0023, but does not move India dense CV; name abbreviations mostly expand legal forms,
  which also raise random-pair overlap (+0.019) → no dense-CV gain. US dense CV (where v2 might help) needs > 31 GB.

## Surprises / bugs
- Bug found by the held-out report: glued web names were learned as "full forms" (`eye`→`eyecare`, `pediatric`→
  `pediatricdental`). Fixed in `27acb15` (≤ 5-letter short forms, compound check) + regression test; the fix is partial
  for names (`new`→`newdelhi` survives because `delhi` is rare in the name vocabulary). E/F dense CV ran pre-fix.
- The laptop (15 GB) OOM-killed the report twice; everything evidence-grade ran on Kaggle.
