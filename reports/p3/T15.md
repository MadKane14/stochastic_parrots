**Findings:**
- **The reverse flood is a tail, and capping it is free.** `rev_cap 20` costs 0.0001 recall and cuts the max from 274 to 50
  (base) or 72 (with the channel, whose own reverse list adds a few). The mean moves by only −0.3.
- **Per-S1 caps below 40 cost real recall:** cap 30 −0.0046/−0.0047, cap 20 −0.0107.
  - At cap 20 the empty-address channel still helps: 0.9709 vs 0.9686.
  - Blocker caps should therefore stay ≥ 40, and the learned pruning stage (~6 cands/S1 after features) is what
    shrinks `candidate_pairs.tsv`.
- **Compute saved by caps:** the pairs to featurize scale with the mean.
  - rev_cap 20 saves ~0.6M pairs (0.8%); cap 40 saves ~1.4M (1.9%).
  - Caps save little compute because the mean is set by the forward k=30, not by the tail.
  - Real savings would need a smaller forward k, which costs recall like the per-S1 caps above.

## What I learned
- **Empty addresses:** at true density these right records are the worst subset (0.854 recall vs 0.979 overall).
  A name-only index over just those records, with S1 normalized over its name, fixes 41% of their misses
  (0.854 → 0.914) at ~1 extra cand/S1.
- **Name × street-word keys:** they recover +0.0073 recall on top of that, at +1.6 cands/S1 and 315 s. They are rare
  even when both words are common, and they survive house-number perturbations and `road`/`rd` drift. That is what
  the compound keys (number × word, name pairs with df ≤ 1000) miss.
- **The reverse flood is a tail:** `rev_cap` 10–20 costs 0.0001–0.0003 recall and cuts the max from 274 to 40–72.
  Per-S1 caps below 40 cost real recall. With the new union, though, cap 20 (0.9847) still beats the old uncapped
  blocker (0.9793).
- **T05's char-gram + address channel is superseded:** T11_eca reached 0.9856 but at ~97M pairs and 4881 s.
