- **(b) New blocker `p3_compound_rc`** (`src/ber/blocking/p3_revcap.py`):
  - It imports p1_compound unchanged. With `rev_cap: null` it gives identical candidates.
  - `rev_cap: N` keeps every forward pair plus the N best reverse-only pairs per S1, which bounds the reverse flood.
  - `p3_chargram` gets the same `rev_cap`.
  - Both expose `candidate_rows_prov` (the reverse-only flag), so `blk_dense.py --rev-caps` emulates every reverse cap
    × per-S1 cap from one run.
  - The per-S1 cap emulates `Pipeline._block`, with ranks recomputed after the reverse cap.
- **Local equivalence tests** on a 40k-S1 real-data sample, all passing:
  - `p3_compound_rc` ≡ p1_compound: same 1,636,549 pairs and scores.
  - The emulated rev caps 1/5/10 equal real `rev_cap` runs: 0 differing pairs.
  - The provenance flag equals "not in a forward-only run".
  - `p3_chargram` output is unchanged by the refactor.
- Grid job `t15d`: caps 20/30/40/50/70 × rev caps 5/10/20/50 for T15_e / e10 / e4 / er / base.
- **New channel `p3_nameaddr`** (`src/ber/blocking/p3_nameaddr.py`; `T15_ena` k=10, `T15_ena5` k=5; job `t15f`):
  - Keys are (name token × address word). The largest miss class left after T11_e is an exact or near-exact name
    with the same street/city words but a truncated house number (`equinox llc | 6600 valley road newburgh in` vs
    `660 valley rd newburgh indiana`), and the T11_e missed-pairs sample is full of it.
  - p1_compound's keys that could link such pairs are either too common to be indexed or broken by the number.
  - Local sample check (low density, plumbing only): it recovers about a third of the sample's remaining misses at
    ~9 s blocking.

## What I learned
- At true density, **right records with an empty address are the worst subset**: 0.854 recall vs 0.979 overall.
  A name-only index over just those records, with S1 normalized over its name, fixes 41% of their misses
  (0.854 → 0.914) at ~1 extra cand/S1.
- **Per-S1 caps are nearly free above 50.** Cap 50 costs 0.0002 recall and cuts the max from 274 to 50. The mean
  barely moves (34.5 → 34.2): the flood is a tail problem, not a mean problem.
