```markdown
### 5.1 Unseen country (France)
France is 15% of the test Source 1 entities (259,452 of 1,732,544) and appears nowhere in the training data, so no
validation split can measure it. Our in-country cross-validation at test density (0.987) and the public leaderboard
(0.979) differ by ~0.008; we traced the gap with label-free diagnostics on the test predictions.

**US and India behave as validated.** On the test set, the statistics we can measure without labels match the
out-of-fold validation of the same countries: house-number conflicts among predicted pairs 6.18% vs 6.07% (US) and
2.16% vs 2.15% (India); empty-address records are assigned at the same rate (≈ 54–60%). Test has about twice the
distractors per S1 of train, but they are easy: the pruned candidate sets have the same size (≈ 6.5–7 per S1).
If US and India score their validation values, the leaderboard implies France ≈ 0.94 for submission 4 and ≈ 0.93 for
submission 7b.

**Why validation could not see France.** Our unseen-country proxy is leave-one-country-out (train on India, score
US and vice versa). Its dominant failure is merging near-copies whose house number is shifted, but France predictions
show almost none of these (0.9% number conflicts), so France fails differently and the proxy misled at least one
decision (the higher threshold, chosen because it helped the proxy). Train-fitted changes also move France 3–5×
more than the known countries: fine-tuned embeddings changed the prediction of 6.8% of France S1 vs 1.4% in US/India;
the threshold change 3.8% vs 1.1%. Models tuned on US/India are least stable where we cannot measure them.

**What we did.** (1) Every component is country-agnostic: statistics are computed within groups of equal country
string, names are romanized from Unicode character names, and nothing branches on a country value. (2) Target-side,
label-free canonicalization (`p4_tcanon`): address abbreviation pairs and junk tokens are mined on each country
group's own records, so France gets `r → rue`, `av → avenue`, `bd → boulevard`, `st → saint` (train would have said
`st → street`) and drops `N°` noise; it rewrites 57% of France S2/S3 addresses and raises true-pair address overlap
on held-out train countries (+0.19 US, +0.05 India) while random pairs barely move (+0.01). (3) The leaderboard is our
only France signal, so we used it for at most three discrete choices (its standard error ≈ 0.0002 makes the selection
optimism negligible). [if adopted: (4) a self-training scorer (`p1_selftrain`) refits on its own confident predictions
of the scored data, adapting to the unseen country without labels; validated by leave-one-country-out
(adapted vs. base on the same folds) with in-country performance not worse.]
```
