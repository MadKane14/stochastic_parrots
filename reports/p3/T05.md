**Candidate-count trade-off (capped union, same qfrac 0.1):**
- The name-only char-gram channels add only +0.0012 (`cg_name`) and +0.0010 (`cg_core`) at cap 50 over `best.yaml`. Name-gram retrieval alone is weak at full density (recall 0.56).
- Adding address tokens to the gram bag (`cg_addr`) makes the channel useful alone (0.8745). The capped union gains +0.0041 at cap 40, 50 and 60 alike.
- Each extra 10 cands/S1 buys about +0.002 to +0.003 recall. Linearly, 0.98 would need ~cap 70–80 for `cg_addr`, above the 50 budget. That's an extrapolation, not measured.
- India stays the bottleneck: 0.9628 vs US 0.9842 at cap 50.

## What I did (≤ 5 bullets)
- Blocker `p3_chargram` (`src/ber/blocking/p3_chargram.py`): IDF cosine over char 3/4-grams of the de-spaced name, optionally plus address tokens. It runs forward top-k and reverse top-k_rev within equal-`country`-string groups. Three configs: `configs/exp/p3/T05_cg_{name,addr,core}.yaml`, all `[p1_compound, p3_chargram]` with `max_candidates: 50`.
- Eval tool `exploration/p3/blk_union.py`: same data, query sampling and metrics as p1's `blk_eval.py` (imported). Each blocker runs in its own process with cached outputs, so peak RAM is the max rather than the sum. The pipeline's `max_candidates` is emulated (best rank over blockers).
- Char-gram selectivity profile: `exploration/p3/gram_df_profile.py` → `exploration/p3/gram_df_profile_out.md`.
- Ran everything as the Kaggle CPU kernel `grootji/ber-p3` (`exploration/p3/kaggle/ber_p3.py`, commit `bb793db`, status COMPLETE). Stages: t01 → blk (4 configs) → prof → val. Logs are in `exploration/p3/kaggle/out_run1/`.

## What I learned
- Name char-grams are weakly selective in 5–7.5M-record country groups (`gram_df_profile_out.md`). With 3+4-grams at max_df 3000, only 4.9 grams per S1 stay indexed (US and India), with ~4.4–5.1k postings per S1. Most of a name's ~38–46 grams are too common to index, which explains the 0.56 channel recall.
- Address tokens rescue the channel (0.56 → 0.87 alone) and give the only meaningful union gain (blk_eval_out.md 21:57).
- In the uncapped union, the reverse direction floods: max 2,274 cands for one S1 (21:57). The per-S1 cap is essential, and the cap choice (40/50/60) dominates recall far more than the choice of name field.

## Script-mismatch observations
1. **qfrac 0.1 inflates the reverse channel ~4×.** `p1_compound` with the same params (k=30, k_rev=3) gives **142.69 mean cands (p95 301, max 1126)** at qfrac 0.1 (21:36) versus **33.37 (max 377) at qfrac 1** in p1's 20:51 run. With only 10% of S1 as queries, every S2/S3 record's reverse top-k_rev lands on the sampled S1s, so each query S1 receives many more reverse candidates, and reverse recall is optimistic (as TASKS item 4 warns).
   - So the **uncapped qfrac 0.1 recalls (0.98+) aren't comparable** with the 0.9699 target baseline. The capped rows are the fairer proxy, but their ranking still sees the inflated reverse pool.
   - Only `--qfrac 1` can settle the ≥ 0.98 @ ≤ 50 target.
2. **Consistency check between tools:** the `best.yaml` cap-50 union from `blk_union.py` (0.9715) is close to p1's `blk_eval.py` forward-only `cmp@50` at qfrac 0.1 (0.9707, run 20:32). That's consistent with the emulated cap plus a small reverse contribution. I did not run `blk_eval.py --config` with two blockers on this box; the `blk_union.py` docstring states it doesn't fit 30 GB, but that's unverified here.
3. **Possible bug in p1's `blk_eval.py` reverse sweep (for the lead; I didn't edit p1 files).** In the 20:51 qfrac-1 table, every `cmp@K + cmprev@K` row is identical to `cmp@K + cmprev@1` (e.g. both 0.9699 / 33.3675 at K = 30, and both 0.9733 / 52.0567 at K = 50). The reverse k doesn't seem to vary in that sweep.
4. **The pipeline caps as emulated:** the dev run's blocking stats show cands mean 42.62, p95 50, max 50 with `max_candidates: 50` (`20260925-221223_…_4dd982`).
5. The "test projection" lines in blk_eval_out.md (e.g. ~6616 s for `cg_addr`) scale total per-process time by 7.9 (the S1 ratio). They're an **upper bound**, not comparable to p1's forward-only projection (~580 s in 20:51). Real test-blocking time needs a qfrac-1 timing.
